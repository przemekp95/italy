# Interface design brief

## Visual thesis

A calm Milanese editorial ledger: warm paper, dark ink, precise rules and one vermilion action accent. The calculator should feel like an accountable calculation sheet, not a generic fintech dashboard.

## Content plan

1. A compact masthead identifies the 2026 Milan estimate and language.
2. The working surface leads with one RAL input, pay-period choice and one dominant `Calcola` action.
3. Results give annual net and average per pay period first, followed by a plain ledger of contributions, taxes and benefits.
4. An expandable calculation trail, persistent assumptions, sources and disclaimer explain the estimate.

## Interaction thesis

- A short result reveal connects the explicit calculation action to the updated totals.
- The calculation trail expands in place with a restrained height/fade transition.
- Keyboard focus and hover share a single high-contrast vermilion treatment; all motion is disabled by `prefers-reduced-motion`.

This is an application surface, so there is no marketing hero or decorative imagery. Information hierarchy is the visual anchor.
